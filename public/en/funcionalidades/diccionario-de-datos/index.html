In detail

What you notice using it every day

It explains the DATA, not the column

Saying that `monto_neto` is "the net amount" explains nothing. The entry says what it includes, what it leaves out and who calculates it.

It says which module WRITES each table

It is the hardest question when something does not add up: knowing which module sets that value saves hunting through the whole system.

A column with two owners does not fail: it stays silent

When two modules write the same field, the result looks right and is wrong. Documenting it is what makes it visible.

Removing a table from the dictionary has consequences

What is documented feeds the report builder. Removing or adding a table changes what can be queried, which is why it is not a cosmetic change.

Platform tables are kept separate

The catalogues Zyntello maintains — countries, currencies, the tax register, ports — are kept apart from your operational data, which is the data that is yours.

It is your insurance against lock-in

A supplier who does not document your data ties you down without saying so. With the dictionary, changing systems is a project; without it, it is starting from scratch.
