1. The essentials, in three sentences

We use five things and none of them is for tracking you. Three are cookies needed so that you can sign in and work without the system throwing you out; the other two are preferences your own browser stores so the page looks the way you left it.

There is no advertising, no profiling and no third-party analytics. We have no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no Hotjar and no tool that measures your behaviour. We do not sell or share browsing data with anyone.

You can delete them whenever you want, from your browser, and we keep working — except the session ones, without which signing in is impossible, just as you cannot get into a house without a key.

2. What a cookie is, in plain words

A cookie is a very small text file that a website stores in your browser and that the same site can read back later. It lets the page remember something from one screen to the next, because without it every page would be the first one: you would sign in and, on clicking any link, you would be out again.

Alongside cookies there is local storage (localStorage), which is similar but with one difference that matters: it never travels to our servers. It stays on your device. We include it here because, although technically it is not a cookie, it is information we store in your browser and you have a right to know about it.

About language. The platform speaks several languages and your choice is saved, but not always in the same place, and the difference matters:

· If you have not signed in yet —the sign-in screen is visible without an account— your choice is saved in two places at once: in the session cookie, for that visit, and in zyntello-idioma in local storage, which survives signing out. The second one is needed because signing out wipes the whole session: without it, anyone who does not read Spanish found the sign-in screen in Spanish every time they came back, and had to hunt for the selector in a language they do not understand.

· If you already have an account, the language is stored in your account and not in the browser. That means it follows you to any device, and clearing cookies does not take it away.

⚠️ Your browser's language does NOT decide for you. We read it only to preselect an option the first time, and nothing else: the browser proposes, you decide. A Dominican in the United States usually has the browser in English and speaks Spanish, and assuming otherwise would put the system in a language they never asked for.

About session length. The 7 days are a maximum; the session ends sooner if you leave, if you sign out from another device or if your company's administrator revokes it. You can see and close your active sessions from My profile → Devices inside the platform.

4. What we do NOT use

We say what there is, but it is worth saying what there is not, because that is what most people assume there is:

  • There are no advertising cookies. We show no ads and we feed nobody else's.
  • There are no third-party analytics. No Google Analytics, no Hotjar, no Clarity, nothing of the kind.
  • There are no social media pixels. Not from Meta, not from LinkedIn, not from TikTok.
  • We do no profiling. We do not build a profile of your interests or your behaviour.
  • We do not sell or share browsing data. To nobody, for any amount.
  • We do not track across sites. What you do outside Zyntello we neither see nor want to see.

And this is verifiable, not a promise. Open your browser's developer tools (F12), go to the Network tab and reload any page of ours. You will see no connections to advertising or analytics domains. We invite you to check: a claim the reader can verify is worth more than one they have to take on trust.

5. Why we do not ask for consent with a banner

You may be surprised not to find the “accept cookies” window that appears almost everywhere. It is not an oversight: it is the consequence of not using the cookies that make it compulsory to ask.

European ePrivacy rules —and the equivalent rules in the countries where we operate— require prior consent for advertising, profiling and third-party analytics cookies. They do not require it for strictly necessary ones, nor for those that store a preference you asked for yourself, such as the light or dark theme.

Everything in the table in section 3 falls into those two categories. Asking you to “accept” something without which the system cannot work would be a false choice, and getting people used to clicking “accept” without reading is precisely what empties consent of meaning.

If this ever changes, we will tell you beforehand. The day Zyntello adds analytics or any tool that requires your permission, the corresponding prior consent will appear —with a real option to refuse— and this Policy will be updated before that tool is switched on, not afterwards.

6. How to delete or block them

You are in charge of what gets stored in your browser. You can delete it or prevent it at any time from the settings:

  • Chrome and Edge: Settings → Privacy and security → Cookies and other site data.
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and site data.
  • Safari: Preferences → Privacy → Manage website data.
  • Android / iOS: Browser settings → Privacy → Clear browsing data.

What happens if you delete them. Preferences are lost and the page goes back to its default appearance, with no further consequences. But if you block cookies from app.zyntello.com, you will not be able to sign in: the system will have no way of remembering that you already identified yourself. That is not a limitation of ours but of how authentication works on the web.

7. Resources served from third parties

A different matter from cookies, and one worth not confusing with them: some of the fonts, icons and interface libraries are served from third-party servers, listed in section 5 of the Privacy Policy.

None of them installs cookies or returns any identifier of yours to us, but the connection exists and your IP address travels in it, as in any request on the internet. We say so because the absence of advertising cookies is not the same as the absence of third-party connections, and a policy that spoke only about cookies would leave out what actually happens when a screen opens.

8. Changes to this Policy

If we change what we store in your browser, we will update this page and the “last updated” date in the header. If the change introduces any cookie that requires your consent, we will ask for it before switching it on.
