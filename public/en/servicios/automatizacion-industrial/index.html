What it solves

Maintenance happens when the machine stops

In many companies maintenance is corrective by default: whatever breaks gets fixed. The real cost isn't the spare part, it's the stopped line, the order that doesn't ship and the overtime to catch up. And since nobody keeps the history, the same failure catches everyone by surprise again three months later.

  • Things get fixed when they break
  • Each machine's history lives in the technician's memory
  • The critical spare part isn't in the warehouse when you need it
  • Processes depend on an operator adjusting them by hand
  • Nobody knows what each machine costs to maintain
  • The preventive plan exists on paper and nobody follows it

What is included

What you actually get

Processes under control

Controllers and sensors that keep temperature, pressure, level or speed on target, monitored from a screen with alarms when something drifts.

Preventive maintenance that actually happens

Tasks scheduled by calendar, hours of use or cycles, which create their own work orders and flag when they fall behind.

Condition-based predictive maintenance

Vibration, temperature or power draw measured on critical equipment, so you step in when the condition calls for it, not when the calendar says so.

A history for every machine

What failed, what was replaced, who did it and what it cost. With that, you can decide whether to repair again or replace.

Spare parts tied to inventory

Every work order draws from the warehouse, and critical spare parts have their own minimum. What the next job needs is there before it's needed.

Maintenance metrics

Mean time between failures, mean time to repair and cost per machine, so you know whether maintenance is working.

In detail

What you notice using it every day

Start with critical equipment

The machines that stop the plant when they fail. That's where preventive and predictive maintenance pay for themselves; for the rest, sometimes the right call is to run them to failure.

History is captured on the spot

The technician logs the job from their phone as soon as it's done. Whatever gets left to write up at the end of the week never gets written up.

The equipment's warranty is respected

Work on power panels is carried out by certified staff, and if the machine is under warranty, it's coordinated with the manufacturer so the job doesn't void it.

Monitoring doesn't replace safety

Emergency stops and machine guards are never touched to automate. Automation adds to safety; it never replaces it.

Connected to the ERP

Work orders, spare parts used and labor hours flow into cost. Maintenance stops being an unexplained expense.

Every sensor gets calibrated

An out-of-calibration instrument triggers false alarms, and an alarm that goes off for no reason teaches people to ignore them. It's calibrated at installation and checked as part of the plan.

Common questions

Questions

What's the difference between preventive and predictive?

Preventive maintenance acts by calendar or by usage; predictive acts on the measured condition of the equipment. They're combined: predictive on what's critical and preventive on the rest.

Do you program the controllers?

Yes. Control logic and monitoring are part of the automation project. If the equipment is under warranty, it's coordinated with the manufacturer so the job doesn't void it.

Does it connect to my inventory?

Yes. Every work order draws spare parts from the warehouse, and critical ones have their own minimum, in your ERP or on the Zyntello platform.

Do technicians have to use a computer?

No. Work orders are received and closed from the phone, with a photo of the job if needed.

How do I know if it's working?

By the metrics: mean time between failures, mean time to repair and cost per machine, compared before and after.

Where do we start?

With the equipment inventory and identifying the critical machines. That's where an hour of downtime costs the most.
