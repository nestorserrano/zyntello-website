What it solves

Decisions get made on what your existing customers say

It is the most accessible sample and the most biased: the people who already buy from you will tell you why they like what you do. The ones who did not choose you — who are the ones that explain why you are not growing — are not in that conversation, and a product or pricing decision made that way comes expensive.

  • You only ask the people who already buy from you
  • The survey is written by whoever wants a particular result
  • Nobody knows whether the sample represents anything
  • The data arrives already processed and cannot be checked
  • The study arrives once the decision has already been made
  • Nobody knows how to read a margin of error

In detail

What you notice using it every day

The sample decides whether the study is any use

A thousand badly distributed responses are worth less than three hundred well designed ones. Size impresses; what matters is who it represents.

The order of the questions matters

What gets asked first conditions what gets answered later. It is a measurable effect, and that is why the questionnaire is ordered deliberately.

Non-response is data too

If one group responds much less, the result tilts without anyone seeing it. It gets measured and corrected, or it gets declared.

The cross-tab says more than the total

A global 60 % can hide a 90 % in one segment and a 20 % in another. The decision is usually right there.

A late study is no use

The deadline is part of the design: if it arrives after the decision, the rigour makes no difference. Better to narrow the scope and arrive on time.

Whoever withholds the data is asking for faith

With the raw data, their conclusions can be checked. Without it, you have to believe them.

Common questions

Questions

How many responses do you need?

It depends on who they represent, not on the number: a thousand badly distributed are worth less than three hundred well designed.

Do you hand over the raw data?

Yes, on top of the report. Anyone who hands over conclusions alone is asking you to trust them; with the raw data they can be checked.

How do you guarantee the fieldwork is real?

With interview verification and supervision. Without quality control, part of a data collection can be fabricated and the analysis will not catch it.

Do you work with the public sector?

Yes: censuses, programme evaluation and opinion studies, with the rigour and traceability it demands.

How long does it take?

The deadline gets set at the start, because it is part of the design: a study that arrives after the decision is no use however rigorous it is.

Do you help interpret it?

Yes, and that is where the value is: the total usually hides what matters, which shows up when you cross-tabulate by segment.
